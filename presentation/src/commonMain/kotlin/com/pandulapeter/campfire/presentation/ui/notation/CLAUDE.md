# :presentation — ui/notation

Drawing the staff notation `:libs:abc:api` renders, in common code with no SVG library: Skia's SVG DOM draws no `<text>`
and ignores `<style>`, and abc2svg writes every music symbol as text in its music font, styled by CSS class.

- **Only the subset abc2svg emits** is read (translate and uniform scale; single-class CSS rules), into plain data
  (`NotationDrawing`, tested). **Anything else gives no drawing**, and the run stays text: better than notation with
  something silently missing. The one non-uniform scale read is the stretched brace of a grand staff (`%%score { … }`,
  which every score a notation program exports has), on a text of music symbols only. Checked against several voices,
  braced staves, decorations, boxes, chord diagrams and grids, string tablature, jazz chords, Clairnote, and jianpu.
- Styles and `<defs>` are gathered across the systems of one rendering, never across renderings.
- `AbcMusicGlyphs.kt` is generated by `tools/fonts/abc_music_glyphs.py`, never edited by hand; the outlines stay
  under the font's OFL.
- Words are drawn in the app's typeface at the CSS's size and weight, placed by baseline and anchor once measured.
  Colours are given only when drawing, so a theme cross-fade lays nothing out again.
- **Chord names are the page's** (`NotationChordNames.kt`, tested): abc2svg moves the notes by the file's
  `{transpose}` plus the reader's sounding transposition, so the staff always sounds; the names are moved by that less
  `nameCapo`, as the lyrics' chords are; a tune's own `%%transpose` adds to both. abc2svg renames any symbol with an
  A–G letter (it read "Do" as D) and annotations would zigzag, so **each drawn chord takes `:libs:chordpro`'s name of
  the symbols it is a transposition of**, in whatever order abc2svg drew them; one matching none (the capo module's
  line) or symbols named apart keeps abc2svg's.
- A tune under a mid-song `{transpose}` is moved by it, each copy of a tune by the key change it stands after:
  renderings are keyed by text and offset (`AbcTune`, `groupStartOffsetsOf`, a walk the PDF shares; a key change inside
  a recalled chorus moves nothing after the recall).
- **Each system's bars are counted** for the scroll's timing (`NotationPlayedLines.kt`, tested): distinct x of the
  `bW` and `bthW` strokes, a bar line before any music closing none, staff by staff, the most any staff plays being
  the system's (an export's voices of uneven bars draw their bar lines apart).
- **The rendering carries its reading** (`NotationReading.kt`, tested): shown titles, the chord names drawn, the words
  verse by verse with syllable marks undone; it is the staff's content description, never the ABC source.
- A tune whose first `T:` is the song's title leaves its title and composer to the page (`AbcTitles.kt`, tested); a
  medley's tunes, named otherwise, keep theirs.
- Painting happens off the main thread (the web's only thread aside) with the rendering, by a painter of the effect's
  own (its own `TextMeasurer`); nothing is painted during composition.
- A song's tunes are rendered for the widest column, each unit `NOTATION_UNIT` times the text size, again when tunes,
  transposition, spelling, width, or text size change; a width or size alone waits 300 ms for the resize or pinch to
  settle, the systems scaled meanwhile. All tunes arrive at once, so sections are measured again once. A rendering
  replacing one the reader changed cross-fades with it (fading in from nothing blinked the tune out) while sections
  glide; the first one, replacing the text, does not.
- **Renderings are kept app-wide** (`NotationCache`: 16 tunes or 4,000 laid-out words, LRU), keyed by everything they
  depend on, so a tune seen before is notation on its first frame. A cold one's arrival moves nothing gradually; a
  rendering that also re-renders a tune is the reader's change and is animated whole. A failed tune is remembered per
  key (the last 64) and drawn as text without rendering again.
- **A tune whose text changed is drawn as the staff it replaced** (paired in order where as many left as came) until its
  own rendering arrives, and cross-fades from it.
- **The engine is warmed** 2 s after the library first holds a song with an `abc` block, on every platform, so a tune's
  first open pays only its rendering.
