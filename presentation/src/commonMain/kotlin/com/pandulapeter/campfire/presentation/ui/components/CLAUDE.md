# :presentation — ui/components

The shared building blocks of the screens.

- `ui/components/` — Material 3 list items (`SongListItem`, `SectionHeader`, `SwitchListItem`, `CheckboxListItem`, ...; a song listed inside a setlist prefixes its one-based place to the title, including when its file is missing. `CenteredSongCardContent` measures the body and actions together so both stay vertically centered however many lines the card has — the body is laid out by hand with a list item's paddings, minimum heights and text styles rather than as a `ListItem`, which pins a three-line item's content to its top and grows it to 88dp, leaving empty card under the labels; `DragHandle` is the grip in front of the overflow button of such a row and narrower than it, so that the button lands exactly where it does on the songs screen — the trailing controls are shifted slightly toward their card edge (`ListItemActions`) — on both list screens the cards end before the fast scroller's column, in every grid column alike — an icon (Material's drag indicator, two columns of three dots) rather than an `IconButton` because the caller is what makes it draggable and because the width it gives up that way is empty space around an icon rather than anything that can be pressed), `SearchableTopAppBar.kt` (`SearchableTopAppBar`, the app bar both list screens use: not a `TopAppBar` but a row of its own drawn *over* the top of the list, because a `TopAppBar` is a `Surface` and would take every touch meant for the pinned header under it. While the search is closed it is transparent and holds only the actions, on a tonal pill (`surfaceContainerHigh`) so that they read as the screen's own rather than as actions of the section whose header they sit on - the same pill the open search's field sits on (`SearchPill`: one rounded rectangle drawn by the bar, interpolated on the search action's spring between where the buttons' row settles while the search is closed and where the field settles while it is open, both taken as the lookahead rectangles of their own state so that neither end is moving and each edge only ever travels one way, from behind the buttons to behind the field and back rather than one fading into the other; the field itself is transparent and only clips to its shape), the list's pinned `SectionHeader` standing where a title would; the header row is as tall as the bar (`LIST_APP_BAR_HEIGHT`, 56dp, a little lower than a `TopAppBar`) and the header itself has nothing behind it: its one line of text (the settings tab labels' style) is laid out as a transparent pill as tall as the bar's, which takes the press up to where a setlist's actions begin and draws none — its name and icon dim while it is held instead, as the song details title does (`PRESSED_HEADING_ALPHA`), and a pointer gets the hand over it — and as a header comes into the pinned place (`SectionHeaderState.pinnedFraction`) its pill, as wide as the cards everywhere else, narrows to the room the buttons leave (`onReachChanged`, measured while the search is closed and settled, and measured before the list — `ListUnderAppBar` — so that a screen's first frame already counts a setlist header's room with it rather than letting the header's actions out of their menu and shrinking them away a frame later) along an eased curve rather than a linear one, and widens and narrows again along the same curve as an opening or closing search moves the list out from under the buttons and back (`AppBarOverlap.coverage`): the pinned header is a bar reaching up to the buttons' pill, its name cut off sooner and a setlist's menu at its end. The cards are what makes room for it: they are faded out entirely under the header's row and fade back in over the 24dp below it (`ListTopFade` in `components/EdgeFade.kt`), so the name is always read against the background; each card masks itself with the part of the gradient it overlaps, since an overlay would fade the sticky headers, which are items of the same list; its strength grows with how far the list is scrolled, over those 24dp, so a list at rest shows its first cards whole (on the setlists screen, how far it is scrolled past the start of the setlist being read, or short of the next one's header, so a setlist brought to the top by a tap on its header shows its description and first song whole, as the first setlist does), and a card takes no press inside the gradient - only the press is consumed, so a drag started there still scrolls the list. `AppBarOverlap` carries that reach and the bar's height to the list, and the fast scroller starts below it so its thumb is never under a button. Opening the search fills the bar in behind the field (`animateAppBarReveal`). On Songs, opening search collapses and fades all section headers on the same spring as `Modifier.underAppBar`, even with an empty query. Section boundaries stay in the grid so incomplete rows keep their columns. A visible card anchors the scroll in screen coordinates during each layout, keeping the active section stationary while later sections close up; closing an unchanged search reverses the collapse and restores the exact original scroll index and offset. Scrolling or changing the results releases the saved anchor; the room kept at the end of the list for it stays until it has scrolled out of sight, the results change or the search is toggled, since dropping it in sight would pull the end of the list up under the finger. `SongSearchScrollAnchor` checks for an active scroll both when the mode changes and before returning a correction during measurement; a transition begun or interrupted during a drag or fling never resumes an old correction once scrolling stops. Its pure state tests cover both races and the exact unchanged round trip. The cards' top fade loses its covered header row on that same transition, ending directly beneath the search bar. On Setlists, the list moves down on the search action's spring and its headers pin under the bar instead. The "New" menu makes way for the field on the same spring (`closedSearchActions`), since making something is not what a search is for; the bar also stays filled in while the list has no header to stand in its place (a placeholder, or a search's ranked results, which come in one headerless group). The bar spans the list alone, never the side panel beside it, so the buttons and the open search stay at the top of the list they act on and the panel reaches the top of the screen; plus `SearchBackHandler`, which closes an open search before a back gesture leaves the screen. It is a navigation event handler (`NavigationBackHandler` over a hoisted `NavigationEventState`) rather than the plain `BackHandler`, which only hears about a gesture once it is over: the whole drag of a predictive back went by with nothing answering it and the search vanished on release. The gesture's progress goes to `SearchState.backProgress` on every frame, and previews the close while the finger is still down — the search action's cross runs back along its timeline towards the magnifier and the field fades and collapses into its end edge the way its exit takes it, with the button offset by exactly as far as the field's start edge has moved, so the two head back to the end of the bar together (`SearchRecession`, held by the bar so both read the same value on the same frame; the offset is layout rather than a drawn translation, so a gesture that closes the search has `animateBounds` carry the button on from where the gesture left it) — and each carries on from wherever the gesture was let go of, on to the end if the search closed and back to where it was if the gesture was cancelled, rather than jumping first; `SearchAction`, the bar button that opens and closes it, which heads the screen's actions while the search is closed and stands at the very start of the bar, where a back button would, while it is open, with the field after it. It is one button travelling across the bar rather than two swapping places, since its mark is turning into the cross as it goes: `movableContentOf` hands it from the actions slot to the navigation icon slot and back, keeping the mark's animation state, and `animateBounds` carries it between them inside a `LookaheadScope` that is only the bar. Each slot keeps a touch target's room for it that grows and shrinks on the same spring (`SearchActionSlot`), which is what moves the title and the other actions out of its way rather than snapping them, and the bounds only animate while the search is opening or closing, so the button does not lag behind a window being resized. Its mark keeps the actions' color in both slots, since the bar draws its navigation icon in a stronger one; and the field itself. The two share one `Box`, the field never leaving it but emptied and laid out with no width while the search is closed, since `animateBounds` only animates a rectangle it has already seen; that also keeps the box as tall as the field, with the title centered in it. The field is a `BasicTextField` laid out by hand rather than `SearchBarDefaults.InputField`, which is fixed at the 56dp of a search bar standing on its own and lets neither its height nor the content padding that decides it be passed in: inside a 64dp app bar that leaves four pixels of daylight above and below, so the field reads as the whole of the bar rather than as something in it. At the 40dp of the actions beside it the clear button comes down to the compact size the header pills' actions use. The pill is what the field never needed while it *was* the title: the bar's close button now sits a few pixels in front of it, and without it the button that empties the field and the button that leaves the search are two bare crosses on one line. The field comes and goes by collapsing into its end edge rather than by sliding, so that its start edge follows the search action from the end of the bar to its start as the search opens and back into the end as it closes, and what it holds is laid out at the full width of the slot, rides the pill's start edge and is cut off at the end - text moving any slower than that edge reads as standing still - so nothing passes under the button that is turning into the close mark as it goes. The pill travels by `animateBounds` on the button's own spring, between the whole field slot and no width at the button's end edge (`CLOSED_FIELD_OFFSET`), rather than being clipped by an animated fraction of its width: a fraction settles a hundredth short of the whole, which is several pixels of the edge jumping on the last frame of a wide window while the button is still moving, and two rectangles on one spring arrive together), `SearchToCloseIcon` (that button's mark — a magnifier whose lens unwinds while its handle grows backwards into one diagonal of a cross, and a second diagonal drawn in behind it. It is a `Canvas` rather than a drawable because it is an animated vector and Compose Multiplatform's resources read only the static kind; the geometry, the stroke, the three stages' offsets and their easings are `avd_search_to_close.xml` transcribed, and they share one linear timeline rather than each getting a spring, since the stages deliberately overlap and three springs would drift apart. The stages keep the milliseconds they were authored in and the whole timeline is *played* over a shorter one: what the drawing carries is the choreography — which stage waits for which, and by how much — which is a set of proportions rather than a length, and a toolbar icon under a finger has to be done well before an icon designed in isolation does), `SegmentedChoice` (offering a selected label only the width its check mark leaves, since Material measures it at the whole segment and cuts it off, and a row whose labels would not all fit beside a check mark has none, the fill alone marking the choice, so "Keyboard" and "Sharps (#)" are whole at 360dp; `isInline` for one control of several in a row, the editor's panes, which drops the mark and the wide side gap and pads its labels less, so the editor's Preview / Előnézet is not ellipsized at 360dp), `ColorChoice` (the theme colors as discs rather than as names, wrapping instead of scrolling so none of them hides behind a gesture; the ones whose color is not the whole of what picks them out carry an icon, which the check replaces while it is selected: the app's own, first and a disc of its two colors' gradient, with the app's mark, and the system's, second where there is one. The order is the preference's own: the app's own, the system's, the colors around the hue circle, and the gray last. The selected one is ringed in its own color, because a mark in the middle of a disc is read as what that color *is* once some of them carry an icon, so the selection has to sit where an icon never can), `SongActions` (`ActionsMenu`, the row of buttons **an overflow menu expands into wherever there is room**: the actions come out of the menu one at a time, in its own order, each an icon button, until none is left and the overflow button goes too, and they go back in the same way as the room shrinks — counted from the largest width the caller hands down (a setlist header keeps 160dp of its name at the width it narrows to once pinned, so it does not trade buttons for a menu as it scrolls into the bar's place, and its pushed copy draws the same icons; the song details bar lets them out only while the title keeps 280dp), and each comes and goes by expanding and shrinking. Neighboring icon buttons reach 12dp into each other's touch targets wherever they stand in a row — a song card's star and dots, the list screens' pill, a setlist header, the song details and editor bars — through `components/ActionOverlap.kt`'s `overlappingAction`, the row padding its ends by half of that so its outer buttons stay where a bar puts them. **What stays behind the tap whatever the room** is part of the rule, since keeping the interface clean matters more than one tap saved: a song card never expands at all (`isExpandable = false`, keeping only its star or drag handle in front of the menu), and a setlist's Edit details and Reorder songs, Archive, Duplicate, Export, Delete, Update file name and the editor's Revert are always menu entries (`ActionsMenuItem.isAlwaysInMenu`) — dangerous, not to be promoted, or rarely wanted — so a menu holding any of them keeps its overflow button. `SongActions` never expands (the song details bar puts its own buttons in front of it, see below), so what can come out is a setlist's Choose songs in its header. On the song details screen, outside read only mode, the editor and the metadata and cover art editors are not in it but in `SongEditingActions`, a second menu before it behind a pencil (`ActionsMenu`'s `icon`), which never expands either: there are as many of them as of the song's other actions together, and the pencil says which menu is which. `SongActions` is the per-song set: edit, "Update file name" where the file is named something other than what its metadata would name it (`Song.canUpdateFileName`, which is also why the entry is absent from most songs), "Export song" (the one way out of the app for a song, whatever the format or the platform, see Export below), and delete — the last only where the song is reached as part of the library, never from a setlist row or a song details screen opened from a setlist (`isDeletable`), since a song read through a setlist is taken out of that setlist rather than out of the library. The Choose setlists sheet is not in a songs screen row's menu: on a row of the songs screen and in the song details app bar, wherever that screen was opened from, it has a `SetlistAssignmentsButton` of its own in front of the overflow button (which the details bar moves into its menu as `setlistAssignmentsAction` where it runs out of room, see `appBarButtons`) (on a song row it reaches 12dp into that button, so the star and the dots are as far apart as a setlist row's grip and dots), since filing songs into setlists is what the library is mostly visited for, whose star, filled where the song is already in a setlist and outlined where it is not (`CampfireViewModel.songFileNamesInSetlists`), cross fades between its outline and its fill while turning clockwise by two fifths of a turn either way, with an anticipate-overshoot easing, a star's five points making the turn end on the outline it started from; a setlist row offers the sheet as `setlistAssignmentsAction` in its menu, after "Remove from setlist" (an archived setlist's rows leave it out, as they leave out every row action), every box free, so that unticking the row's own setlist takes the row off the screen behind the sheet as it happens; a song details screen opened from a setlist offers it with that setlist's box shown but disabled (`DialogType.SetlistPicker.setlistFileName`), because taking the song out of the setlist being read would pull the screen out from under the reader. Every song row and the song details app bar reach the list through `SongActions`, and they get the same entries in the same order otherwise - which is an overflow button on every platform wherever the actions do not all have a button of their own - a gesture announces itself to nobody, so touch cannot be left with one - and **every overflow button in the app opens a dropdown**, touch included, since that is what an overflow button promises. A long press on a song row of the songs screen opens that very dropdown on touch platforms, hanging from the row's button (the row holds the menu's `OverflowMenuState`), since the same entries shown two ways would read as two different things; it is a shortcut for the reader who knows about it rather than the way in, which is why the button is there whatever is pointing at it), `SetlistActions` (the per-setlist actions at the end of its `SectionHeader` pill: edit, Choose songs, reorder songs (Done reordering while the mode is on, offered for an unarchived setlist of two songs or more), duplicate, archive or unarchive, "Export setlist", delete - Choose songs is the only one that becomes a header button where the header has room, every other entry always staying behind the overflow button, and all but one of them lead somewhere else anyway. It starts with edit and then the assignments, the way a song's menu does, so the two read alike. "Edit" is the rename and the one place a setlist's description and date are written, since the three are the whole of what the user gets to say about one; the date is a read-only field that opens Material's date picker, so there is no typed text that could fail to be a day), `OverflowMenu` (the dropdown behind every overflow button - the song and setlist menus and the editor's revert - which is also what counts the open ones, since a menu keeps that inside the composition and the desktop Escape handler is outside it; its entries act through `OverflowMenuState.select`, which closes the menu and runs the entry once per opening, because a `DropdownMenu` stays on screen and tappable through its exit animation and a double tap on Export would export twice), `ImportProgress`, `SortMenu` (the order of either list, a popup of radio rows headed by what it sorts ("Sort songs", "Sort setlists") behind the app bar's sort action on both list screens and at the end of both picker sheets' headers, which closes with the choice; `SongSortMenu` and `SetlistSortMenu` are the two, shared by a screen and its sheet), `SongFilters` (the song list's filters, the order not among them), used both as side panel and bottom sheet, and as neither where the library offers nothing to filter by — no tag, and at most one language (`CampfireViewModel.hasSongFilters`) — since there is no longer anything in them that is always there; the filter action leaves the bar the same way; `ControlsSidePanel` holds those controls beside the songs list, under the app bar and next to the list, the way the rail is at the other end; only the list feeds the bar's nested scroll, since the bar is kept in step with the list's own position. `SongListItem` names the key the song sounds in next to the artist rather than next to the title, which is the longest thing on the row and the one that must never be pushed out of sight, in the accent color the song details header keeps for what is played: `{key}` moved by the `{transpose}` the file opens with, by the transposition of the listing it is read in and by the fret it is capoed at there (`SongRenderer.renderKey`), which is why one song is in one key on the songs screen and in another inside a setlist that plays it differently. The capo is in it because this is what the band hears: a capo raises every fretted chord without renaming one of them, so a card is the one place that says so - the song details screen's Transposition control names the written key instead. The tempo follows it (`EffectiveTempo.displayedBpm`, the setlist's entry, the library's override or the file's `{tempo}`, and nothing at all where only the metronome's default is left), in the duration's muted color rather than the accent one, since a second emphasized value beside the key halves what the key is there for. Both are worked out from the metadata the library scan already read rather than by parsing the file, since a list holds the whole library and none of its lyrics. A song with no chords says "Lyrics only" in the key's place instead, and a song that names no artist starts the line with the note itself. Whatever stands there is crossfaded rather than swapped (`SongCardNote`), since it changes under the reader: a transposition or a capo renames the key, a stepper moves the tempo, and the chords switched off empty the key's place altogether. The Chords switch off empties the place altogether — no key, and no "Lyrics only" either, since that marker only tells one song from another while the others are showing chords — the way the switch takes the transposition control off the viewer. The song list has priority over the panel: `hasRoomForSidePanel` only shows it when at least two `ListColumns` columns still fit beside it, the fast scroller's column counted (~1070dp of screen width next to the rail), otherwise the same controls open in a bottom sheet from an app bar action. **In a window under 480dp wide** a song card gives its text 8dp of padding instead of the keyline — and the section headers, a setlist's countdown and its description move in with it (`songCardTextKeyline`), so the list's text starts on one line — the dot between the artist and the key a 16dp box instead of 24dp, and its title two lines instead of one; the touch targets of its buttons stay as they are. A list's columns keep their 360dp minimum on a phone held sideways too, where two would each be narrower than the one in portrait. The Setlists screen's columns are at least 416dp (`MIN_SETLIST_COLUMN_WIDTH`, `ListLayout.setlistColumnCount`), since its cards also carry the slot number, the key, the tempo and the duration, and at the songs' 360dp a laptop's maximized window cut all of them short across four columns.
- `ui/components/Languages.kt` — what a language is called, and the only part of the language feature the app ships: `LANGUAGE_CODES` (the ~620 codes the picker offers — ISO 639-1 plus the three letter ones 639-1 never covered, since no browser will enumerate languages) and `languageLabel(code)` / `languageName(code, appLanguageCode)`, which ask the platform for the name in the language the app is set to — `currentLanguage` is Compose state, so a chip follows the settings screen without a restart — and capitalize it, since CLDR writes `angol` rather than `Angol`. The English name stands in where the app's own language has none (CLDR thins out fast past the first few hundred languages, and a picker that lost a row when the app was switched to Hungarian would be worse), and only a code no platform can name at all is shown in capitals as the file wrote it. `pickableLanguages` is the picker's list: what can be named, ordered by that name through `NormalizeTextUseCase` so accents sort where a reader expects them — with the languages the library already sings in ahead of the rest, since the next song to be filed is far likelier to be in one of that handful than in any of the six hundred the library has never held. `SongLanguage.UNKNOWN` is the one code that is named from the string resources instead.
- `ui/components/CoverArtImage.kt` / `ProvideCoverArtImageLoader.kt` — the covers, drawn by Coil 3 (`coil-compose`, and no network artifact: the bytes come
  from `GetCoverArtUseCase`, which keeps the offline copy and goes through the one HTTP client in
  `:data:source:remote`). `ProvideCoverArtImageLoader`, called once by `CampfireApp`, which resolves the use case there
  (`koinInject`, where the composition is) rather than when Coil first asks, sets the singleton loader up with
  a fetcher and a keyer for `CoverArt(url)` and no disk cache of Coil's own, since the use case's copy is that on all
  four platforms; Coil keeps what it decoded in memory for scrolling, and crossfades only what did not come from there.
  `CoverArtImage` is a cover on its own, whole, over a placeholder of its own size (a tinted square with the album
  icon), which is what shows while it loads and where it never does: the room is taken from the first frame, so an
  image arriving in a list being scrolled moves nothing around it, and the image crossfades in over the placeholder. A
  song card (`SongListItem`'s `coverArtUrl`, on the Songs and the Setlists screen alike, never on a missing entry's
  row) puts it at 44dp with small corners at the start of its body, 12dp before the text, next to the title and the
  artist line only and as tall as the two, while the labels under them start from the keyline beneath it, 8dp under
  the cover (`CenteredSongCardContent`'s `labelsContent`). The song details screen puts it at 40dp in its app bar, in front of the title and the artist of
  the song on screen, and counts it in the width the Choose setlists button is let out of the menu by whenever any song of
  the pager has one, leaving it out where that would leave the title less than 160dp; it is part of the title there, so a tap on it scrolls to the top, or opens the About the song
  sheet once the song is already there, like the rest of the bar, with no
  press drawn. The editor's bar draws it the same way, from the text as it is typed, so
  a cover written or changed by hand is seen before it is saved: an address only once the typing has paused for 500 ms
  (`rememberSettledCoverArtUrl`), since every half-typed one that happens to be valid would be a download of its own,
  and with no press, since the search is in the editor's menu. The About the song sheet and the preview's card put it
  at 96dp at the start of their Song details group, the detail tiles flowing beside it (the group is there for a cover
  alone too), the card's following the text the same settled way; where the cover search is offered (outside
  performance mode, cover art on) a tap on it opens that search, which is why the header's cover button is there only while no cover is (fading and
  sliding in and out as one arrives or goes). The lyrics'
  header draws none. Both follow
  `UserPreferences.isCoverArtEnabled`, the Features tab's "Cover art" switch, which leaves every cover unfetched and
  undrawn when it is off; performance mode keeps them, since a cover changes nothing.
- `ui/components/SaveShortcut.kt` — `Modifier.saveShortcut`, Ctrl / Cmd + S (not with Alt, which AltGr arrives as), on every surface that saves: the editor, the export screen (Save, while the button is there and not counting pages), the editor's unsaved changes question, and every `CampfireBottomSheet`, where it presses the `BottomSheetConfirmButton` in the header (`LocalSheetSaveShortcut`, which the button sets while it is there; the library deletion's opts out with `isSaveShortcut`), doing nothing while that is disabled or the sheet is closing. A key only travels along the focus path, so each of them holds the focus itself as it opens with a bare focus target, which draws nothing and brings up no keyboard: the sheet takes it a frame in and only where nothing in it has it by then, so a form's first field keeps the caret. The key is consumed whether or not anything is saved, so that it never reaches what is under a sheet.
- `ui/components/DelayedLoadingIndicator.kt` — the loading indicator of the song details and editor screens, which only fades in once a read has taken 300ms: those read one local file, which is nearly always done before an indicator would have been seen, and flashing one for a few frames on every page is noise rather than feedback. The song lists keep the plain indicator, since the launch screen covers their first read.
- `ui/components/TagFlowRow.kt` / `TagPill.kt` — `TagFlowRow` and `TagPill`, the one place a tag is drawn: under the artist in a song row (`SongLabels`, kept to a single line that scrolls sideways, so a song filed under a dozen labels cannot make its row taller than the others and none of them is cut off; the row fades out towards whichever edge it continues past, each fade as wide as what is left to scroll that way, masked with `BlendMode.DstIn` in an offscreen layer so that it fades to the card rather than punching through it — and leaving off whatever every song in the library carries, `CampfireViewModel.labelsOnEverySong`: a tag on every song tells one song from no other, and a library sung in one language has nothing to mark a song with. That is counted over the whole library rather than the filtered list, since a tag filter narrows the list to songs that all carry the tag, and the rows would lose the very label they were narrowed by). A tag or a language there is a shortcut to its own chip in the song filters (`toggleTagFilter`, `toggleLanguageFilter`), keeping the tapped row in place rather than sending the list to the top, drawn selected while it is on — a row has no chip that writes the song's tags, which is the details overflow menu's business; the setlists screen leaves both off its rows altogether (`shouldShowLabels`), since a setlist is not a view of the library either could narrow. Tags and languages in the song details card are read-only; only their dialogs edit them. `SongLabels` draws the song's languages there too, after the tags (the order of the details header and of the filters) each kind carrying the mark the details card's chips and the overflow menu's entries do (`ic_label` for a tag, `ic_language` for a language) — a pill reading "Magyar" is otherwise either, and a list gives no other way of telling the two apart. The filter chips of `SongFilters` are Material filter chips instead, since there the selected state is the point — drawn by `CountedFilterChip` itself rather than by `FilterChip`, which puts its state layer around the label instead of around the chip, so only a band hugging the text lit up under the pointer. Every color, the border, the shape and the height still come from `FilterChipDefaults`; what the chip is built out of here is a `selectable` whose `indication` is a node of its own, placed outside the chip's padding so the state layer covers what can be clicked, under a `clip` that rounds it (nothing else binds it, which is what let it spill past the corners on the web). `TagFilters` (in `SongFilters.kt`) shows the first tags in the chosen order — most used, or alphabetical, switched by the `LabelSortingToggle` right after the group's title, whose icon shows the order the chips are in; `LanguageFilters` and the tag and language dialogs (at the end of their title row) carry the same toggle on the same preference, and "Unknown" stays last either way — and keeps the rest behind "Show all": the set of tags is whatever the library grew, and a hundred of them must not push the languages off the bottom of the panel. **Where the fold falls is decided by the room, not by a count** (`FilterGroupsLayout.kt`): `FilterGroupsLayout` shares the height of the panel or the sheet between the two groups' chips, measuring everything else first and asking each `CollapsibleChipFlow` for its height whole and collapsed as far as it goes (two lines, the selected chips and the toggle) as its intrinsic heights, so one measure pass decides it; `chipBudgets` fills the groups like vessels, a short group shown whole next to a long one and two long ones cut to about the same height, and a flow given less than it needs shows the lines that fit, the first chips in order plus every selected one, and "Show all" under them. Everything fits whole where there is room, and nothing is cut below the minimum where there is not — the content scrolls. A chip whose place changes — the order toggled, or a count that moved it past another — travels to its new place (`animateBounds` in the filter column's one `LookaheadScope`, inside the scroll, each chip composed under its key), as the song picker's chip rows do the same way. A group opened with "Show all" is shared out as if it were collapsed, so opening one never closes the other, and the "any / every" choice is left out of the sharing (`FilterSlot.TRANSIENT`), since taking its height from the chips would hide some of them under the finger that just selected the second one. In a sheet the height is the sheet's at its tallest (`BottomSheetContentScope.uncoveredTopInset`): Material pads a sheet by the part of the top inset its edge has come into, so the height offered changes with its offset, and content cut to that height would decide the sheet's height and so its offset again — a layout pass on every frame that never settled. A selected tag is always among the visible ones, and the "any / every" choice only appears once two are on, because with one it would mean the same either way. What empties either group sits at the end of that group's title (`FilterSectionTitle`) rather than under its chips: it comes and goes with the selection, and a button of its own would have moved everything below it — the height of the sheet included — every time a filter was turned on. The row is built to keep the height of the plain title it replaces, so the action can never decide it. Under the last group a "Reset all filters" button empties both at once (`clearSongFilter`), offered for as long as the filter action carries its badge (`isSongFilterActive`) and left out of the sharing (`FilterSlot.TRANSIENT`) like the "any / every" choice; "Show all" stays under the chips, since it belongs to the list it ends and appears with the room the chips have rather than with what is selected. Opening and closing a group is animated both ways: the room is shared out in the lookahead pass only, where each group decides its chips, and the approach pass animates the group's bounds, the groups below following; a chip that joins or leaves fades as the group's edge passes it (`chipTransitionAlpha`), and the toggle rides that edge, its label crossfading. `LanguageFilters` sits under it and is laid out the same way — a library that sings in a handful of languages shows them whole, and one gathered from all over the world is cut down like the tags — with an "any / every" question of its own, since a song can carry several languages. The whole group only appears once the library holds more than one language, counting the songs that declare none as one of them: below that there is nothing to choose between. Both groups draw the same `CountedFilterChip`, which is a `SelectableChip` (`SelectableChip.kt`) with a count in it; the metronome's time signature and sound chips are `SelectableChip`s too, so no chip in the app is a Material `FilterChip`.
- `ui/components/FastScroller.kt` — the only scrollbar in the app, on the song list and the setlists: a draggable thumb and, only while the thumb is pressed, an animated bubble with the letter of the section at the top — on the song list alone, since a setlist is named by whatever somebody called it and has no letter to be found by. Its `FAST_SCROLLER_WIDTH` (24dp) touch column overlays the grid's end padding, so cards and their overflow buttons end before it while full-width sticky headers continue underneath it. This keeps the touch target clear of every card, including the inner columns of a wide grid. The column is kept whether or not the list is scrollable, since one that came and went would reflow every row as the list grew past the screen. The whole column is the touch target, and it reaches on over the last column's 8dp outer margin up to the cards' edge, since nothing in that margin can be pressed; it stops 8dp short of either end of the list like everything the scroller draws: a press anywhere on it centers the thumb under the pointer and carries on as a drag, the way the fast scroller of a contacts app answers a touch on its edge — at once for a mouse, but for a touch only once the finger has been held there for a long press or has set off more vertically than horizontally past the touch slop (`awaitThumbPress`): the column runs down the edge Android's back gesture starts from, the system takes a swipe over only after it has travelled, and a thumb that jumped on the press moved the list under every back gesture made there, so a sideways swipe is left alone, and a tap jumps the thumb only once the finger lifts — a lift the system has taken over for its back gesture arrives already consumed, and is left alone too — while a press on the thumb or within 8dp of it never makes it jump; a faint track fades in behind the thumb while the column is hovered or dragged, so its reach can be seen. Only the circular bubble reaches out of the column: it is level with the thumb, at the height of the finger, centered under the divider between the closed app bar's buttons and its new item menu (`CLOSED_SEARCH_ACTIONS_DIVIDER_END_INSET`), and stays inside the track near either end. The thumb and the track fade by the alpha they are drawn with rather than through a `graphicsLayer`: an offscreen buffer the size of the column would cut the bubble off, and the web build does not redraw a `ModulateAlpha` layer whose alpha alone changed, so the thumb stayed invisible there until a hover redrew it. Scroll position is estimated from the average visible item size; the pinned sticky header is excluded from that estimate, since it is listed among the visible items out of order. Visible on every platform for as long as the list is scrollable. The list screens' app bars have fixed color and elevation, so scrolling the fast scroller does not alter them.
- `ui/components/CampfireTopAppBar.kt` — the app bar of the song details and the editor: flat, in the background color, never tinting or lifting. In a short window the song details screen hands it an `enterAlwaysScrollBehavior`, so it leaves as the song is dragged down and comes back as it is dragged up or reaches the top — the bar and the status bar are a third of a phone on its side — while the step buttons, the keys and the pedal leave it where it is, since they do not scroll through the nested scroll. The pages decide their grid against the height they have with the bar and the metronome panel at rest (`isAppBarSettled`, `settledHeight`), while their rows follow the live viewport, so a bar on the move searches no grid; the editor's is `isCompact`, a 48dp title row, while its keyboard is up. Nothing in the app has an elevated bar or a divider under a bar or tabs; what scrolls under them fades out into them instead (`fadingTopEdge` in `components/EdgeFade.kt`, a gradient over the top 24dp of the scrolling container's viewport, as strong as the content is scrolled over those 24dp; every fade over one known opaque color paints that color over the content's edge rather than masking it, which would take a viewport-sized offscreen buffer on every frame drawn while scrolled — screens on the background color, sheets on `sheetContainerColor()` (`dialogs/CampfireBottomSheet.kt`), the color has to be the one directly behind the container, and the settings pager's swipe fade (`fadingLeftEdge`, its strength read while drawing) — and the transparent mask is kept only for the alert dialogs, the list screens' cards (`ListTopFade`), sideways-scrolling rows (`fadingUnderStartOverlay`) and the overscroll pull; `fadingVerticalEdges` fades both ends the same way, as far as there is content past each, for a list inside a dialog, which has no bar above it or screen edge below it to say it goes on — the What's new dialog's list, above its button; never a sheet's content, which ends at the bottom of the window or at the keyboard; the list screens' cards fade the same way per card, see `ListTopFade` below). A screen with more controls than one row holds puts the rest in `bottomContent`, which is drawn inside the same surface (the editor's toolbar). Each song row sits on a low tonal card with rounded corners and an 8dp gap in either grid direction; the outer columns keep their 8dp screen gutter, and a dragged setlist card lifts above its neighbors. The lists' sticky section rows (`SectionHeader`) have no background and no divider, the cards being faded out under the pinned one instead, and only the header's content takes a touch, and neither it nor a setlist's menu button in it takes the focus: a lazy grid keeps a focused item placed after it has scrolled away, and a header held that way stops the grid drawing the headers it pins after it. As the next header pushes the pinned one away, a visual copy outside the grid crosses the grid's top edge, under the transparent app bar, so the grid's top clip cannot cut it off; its content lags behind the row and fades with the visible height, and it keeps the pinned width it had in the bar's place rather than widening again as it leaves. The row extends behind the fast scroller, so the pill's end and the app bar's reach are measured from one edge, and the pill never reaches past the cards' end. Their text is one line in the settings tab labels' style (with a second in the description's style: a setlist's countdown where one was asked for, then, after a ` · `, the total of its songs' durations wherever one of them has one, with a `+` where some have none or are missing, `SetlistTotalDuration.kt` — the pill is as tall as the bar's and holds both), and leaves the setlist menu its space at the pill's end. The section being read stays named at the top of the list with its cards passing under the row, and the setlist header's menu stays in reach however long the setlist is; clicking one scrolls back to the start of that section, which is the index the list hands a sticky header's content.

## Scrolling performance

Overscroll is Compose's own: Android's stretch, iOS's bounce, and none on the desktop and the web, where Compose
provides no effect. Foundation only applies it while there is something to scroll, so the scrolling containers that
can be short — the list screens, the sheets, the settings, the song and the editor — also take
`bounceScrollableContent` (`ui/platform/ContentOverscroll.kt`, or `bounceVerticalScroll` / `bounceHorizontalScroll`),
which hands the drags of content that fits to the same effect, after offering them to the parents first, so a bottom
sheet whose content fits is still dragged down by it. Content that fits and is pulled up is carried to the top edge of
its viewport, under a sheet's header or an app bar, with its scroll position still at zero, so the container's own
`fadingTopEdge` stays off: the vertical form draws a top fade of its own over the stretched content, as strong as the
finger has pulled it up and settling back on a spring as the effect relaxes. The two list screens fade their cards
under the pinned header rather than at the grid's edge, so they hand their `ListTopFade`'s `OverscrollPull` in
instead: the cards' fade then grows with the pull, and its gradient is moved down the cards by as much, since the
stretch carries their drawing up with them.

`ListTopFade` caches an inverse gradient and uses `DstOut` inside its existing offscreen layer, translating the drawing
rather than rebuilding a shader for every card position. `FastScroller` consumes a conflated channel once per frame so new pointer events cannot cancel
the frame wait or a scroll in progress; animation values are read while drawing. `SongStepper` scans offsets without
sorting or allocating lists per scroll update and derives button availability independently from the target position.
