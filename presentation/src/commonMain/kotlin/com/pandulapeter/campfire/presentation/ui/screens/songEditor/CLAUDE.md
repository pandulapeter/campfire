# :presentation — ui/screens/songEditor

The song editor.

- `screens/songEditor/` — the raw ChordPro editor, opened over the song with the same horizontal slide as the song details screen. Its app bar starts with Close rather than Back. The whole bar is one surface (`CampfireTopAppBar`'s `bottomContent`), and it is four rows deep on a narrow window:
  - the **title row**, holding what acts on the file — undo/redo from `TextFieldState.undoState`, Save, and an overflow menu;
  - a row with the **transposition stepper** and the **Edit / Preview / Split** choice. These two are together because they are the two controls here that do not write at the caret, which is why they are also the two that stay when the rest leaves. Split is only offered where two panes fit (`WindowSize.EXPANDED`) but is what the choice starts at, and a window that narrows only *shows* Edit instead of it, so widening the window again goes back to both panes. Split's preview stacks the song in a single column however wide its pane is (`SongLyrics`' `isSingleColumn`), since columns that the next keystroke reshuffles hide the line being typed; Preview on its own lays the song out as the song details screen does. The choice is drawn whenever the row is, including on a window wide enough that it has all three options, so that the stepper is never alone in a row of its own; its segments are inline and add no padding at their sides, which is what keeps "Preview" whole beside the stepper at 360dp. The insertion rows below it fold once as the keyboard appears (see below), and **where the keyboard leaves less than 240dp** of the window (`MIN_ROOM_FOR_CONTROL_ROW`, a phone on its side) this row gives way too, animating away while its Shortcuts chevron moves into the title row, so the rows can still be opened while typing; only its visibility follows the keyboard, so the pane and the fold the user chose come back with it;
  - two rows of `EditorToolbar.kt`, a button for every directive `ChordProParser` understands and the app then shows, so that a format whose vocabulary only exists in its own documentation can be written without knowing it by heart. They are split into what writes what the song **is** (title, key, tags — what a file opens with, usually filled in once: the directives a song holds once first, then Tag, Language and Link, then Chord shape) and, under it, what writes the **song** (chords, the seven sections the app has a name for, tablature and grids, comments, and last the changes from where they stand: Key change, Tempo and Time signature), so the row being scrolled through is always the short one. Tab and Grid are a group of their own between the sections and the comments, because that is what they are: a display mode that opens inside a section rather than a section of its own.
  - Two kinds of directive are deliberately absent, both still read without complaint: `{new_song}`, since the editor only ever holds one song, and `{new_page}` / `{column_break}`, since Campfire flows sections into columns at whatever size the window is and a hand written break would decide nothing. `{transpose}` is offered as **Key change**, in the second row's last group, written at the caret like a comment: further down a song it is the key change the page then names (see `SongKeyChange.kt`), and it is not among the header's buttons, since the one a song opens with shifts every chord away from what the file spells out, which the details screen's transposition and the editor's transpose action do while saying what they did. The rows scroll horizontally rather than wrapping, and their buttons take no focus, or the keyboard would close under the toolbar on every tap and the insertion would land where the caret used to be. Everything in braces is inserted on a line of its own, which is the only way ChordPro reads it as a directive. The metadata row is the one that does not write at the caret at all: a directive describing the song is valid anywhere in the file and belongs in none of those places but the header, so `:chordpro`'s `ChordProHeader` picks the line (in the order the row itself lists them, and without moving anything the user has arranged their own way) and the caret follows it there, ready for the value. Those buttons also go out of reach once the file carries what they write — a song has one title and came out in one year — which leaves Tag and Language as the two that can be tapped again and again, since a song has as many of either as it carries — and Tempo and Time signature, which go into the header while it has no line of their kind and otherwise at the start of the caret's line in the body, as a change from there on (`ChordProHeader.insertChangeable`), selecting the header's value where the caret is in the header; what the file declares is read from the text as it is typed, so a directive written by hand disables its button as well. The two rows fold away behind a chevron at the end of the row holding the transposition and the pane choice, since together they are taller than what a phone has left for the text once the keyboard is up: a window whose shorter side is under 600dp opens the editor with them folded, anything larger with them out. The shorter side rather than the width class, because a phone turned sideways counts as a wide window and has the least height of all. On these small screens, and in short windows, the rows collapse once when the keyboard appears. Closing the keyboard does not restore an earlier expansion state. The chevron stays available while typing — in the control row, or in the title row where that row has given way. Where the window is wider than the compact class the chevron carries a "Shortcuts" label, naming the rows rather than the action so it keeps one width as the chevron turns. Over a preview the chevron is disabled rather than hidden, so the segments beside it keep their width. While the keyboard is up in a short window the title row is 48dp (`CampfireTopAppBar`'s `isCompact`), and the field's lines are 18sp apart without the letters getting any smaller, to keep more room for text above a landscape keyboard, with the status bar taken away too on Android (`CompactKeyboardEffect`).
  - **The field is in the reader's notation** (`UserPreferences.ChordSpelling.notation`, `CampfireViewModel.editorNotation`)
    and the file always in the standard one — except under a numbering, where the field is in letters
    (`Notation.forTyping`) and only the preview is in numbers: a field in numbers would make correcting a wrong `{key}`
    move every chord at Save, and a key half typed would leave every number with nothing to count from. Otherwise `SongRenderer.editorTextOf` (`rendering/SongRenderer.kt`, tested, handed the view model's `editorNotation`
    explicitly) converts the file's text as the field is opened, reverted or made to
    follow its file, and Save converts the field back (`fileTextOf`), so a German reader types `[B]` for B flat in any
    key and the file says `Bb`. Every comparison of the field with the file — what is unsaved, the draft reopened on
    launch — is made in the field's notation, while the stored draft is in the file's, so it means the same chords
    whatever the notation is by the time it is reopened. A chord typed in a spelling the notation has a second one for
    (a German `Bb`, a `♭`) is written the one way, and a save brings the field to what was written, through the same
    `editorTextEdits` the metadata sheets use. The preview parses the field in its notation, the summary reads it
    that way too (`editorSummaryCache`) and the stepper shows the key in it (`editorKeyOf`); the transposition converts
    the field to the standard notation, transposes it and converts it back. Settings cannot change the notation under
    an open editor, since selecting a top level screen clears the stack.
  - The stepper is the details screen's own `TextTranspositionControls`, except that transposing here rewrites the file rather than changing how it is read: there is no amount and nothing to reset to, so it shows the song's key, or a `?` where the file names none. Either a `{key}` or a chord is enough to enable it — a key says what the song is in and is worth moving before a chord has been written under it, and a chord chart with no key is still a chord chart. The title is `ChordProMetadata.displayTitle` over the text as it is typed and the line under it the artist, with the cover in front of them (see `CoverArtImage.kt` above), both from one summary of the text (`ChordProSummaryCache`, which parses again only for a keystroke that could change it) that also answers whether there is anything to transpose; the save state is the Save button's own enabled state and is not spelled out. A transposition keeps the caret and the selection next to the text they were at (`ChordProTransposer.transposedOffset`), since every chord name that grows or shrinks above them would move them otherwise; Prettify likewise keeps both ends of the selection on their matched lines (`ChordProPrettifier.prettifiedOffset`), and whether it would change anything is only decided while the menu is open (`ActionsMenuItem.isEnabledInMenu`), never per keystroke; an edit from the menus' sheets, a save's respelling and a file followed while untouched keep both next to their text too (`editedOffset`: line by line where the line count is unchanged, otherwise across one changed stretch); a revert has nothing to map by and keeps the offset.
  - The preview shows the text as written: none of the reader's transposition, whether the song was opened from the library or a setlist (the file's own `{transpose}` still applies, being part of the text), as it ignores the Chords and Metronome switches — so the field, the stepper and the preview name the same chords and the same key, the one exception being a numbering, whose preview counts the chords the field names in letters.
  - The preview's About the song card has the sheet's edit buttons (Edit song details, Manage tags, languages and links, `rememberSongInfoEditing`, so the card is there even for a song that says nothing about itself yet), and the overflow menu keeps the cover art, a link to the ChordPro directives reference on chordpro.org (always in the menu, for what the Shortcuts write, a `{define}` above all) and the revert — and holds those four as well (`songInfoEditingActions`, the same callbacks) in every pane, so they are there while the preview, and with it the card, is out of sight; they open the same bottom sheets on a snapshot of the text being typed (`toEditorSong`) — but there they edit that text rather than the file (`DialogType.SongEdit.target`, a `SongEditTarget.EditorDraft` rather than a `SongEditTarget.File`): the change reaches the screen through `CampfireViewModel.editorTextEdits` and goes through the field's own editing (`EditOnRequest`), one more step of the undo history that Save writes with the rest, since nothing but Save ever writes the file the editor is open on. Such a sheet is not taken down when the file leaves the library, since the draft is still there.
  - Export and Delete are not here: they belong to the song list and the viewer. Revert is, last in the overflow menu and behind a confirmation (`DialogType.RevertChanges`) — in the menu rather than a button of its own, because throwing away everything typed since the last save is not something to end up in by mistapping the button next to Save. It is answered by the screen rather than by the view model — the text goes back through the field's own editing, so a revert is one more step of the undo history instead of the end of it. In a document of more than 50,000 characters a whole-document rewrite (a transposition, a revert) starts the undo history over first, since each is recorded as the whole text twice.
  - The screen itself is a `BasicTextField` over a `TextFieldState`, scrolled by containers around it rather than by its own `scrollState`, which has no way to hold room after the last line: a `verticalScroll`, which hands the field its viewport height as the minimum so a short song is still a field down to the bottom of the pane, and inside it a `horizontalScroll`, since the field never wraps a line — the staff lines of a tab only stay in their columns while each is one line on screen — which measures it against an unbounded width and still hands it the pane's as the minimum. Both follow the caret because the field asks its ancestors to bring it into view. The margins and the room after the last line (the navigation bar and 32dp) are the field's own decorator, so a press in them places the caret, the focus never scrolls a margin out of sight, and the text runs edge to edge under the navigation bar, as every list does, clearing it once scrolled to the end. Only the keyboard is taken off outside the scroll, once it reaches above the navigation bar it covers (`EditorFieldPadding`, read while the field is laid out), so the viewport ends at the keyboard and a caret brought into view is above it; the preview's scroll room is read the same way (`PaddingValues.only`), while the height the preview's sections are laid out against leaves the keyboard out: it only covers the lower part of the preview, so it moves neither pane's composition nor the preview's columns as it slides. Both scroll states and the preview's are held by the screen next to the `TextFieldState` rather than by the panes, since a pane is composed from scratch whenever Edit, Preview and Split change places and would otherwise go back to the first line every time, an explicit Save action (and Ctrl / Cmd + S, heard by the whole editor, which takes the focus itself as it opens and whenever its panes change, since nothing in it is focused before the text is clicked or while only the preview shows — except that a field that was being typed in keeps the focus across a change that still shows it, such as a rotation or a resize across Split's width, so the caret and the keyboard stay) which is the only thing that ever writes the file, and a live preview of the same `SongLyrics` the viewer uses (parsed and split into sections away from the main thread once the typing pauses, the previous rendering staying on screen until the new one is ready), side by side where the window has the room for it and one at a time otherwise — and the insertion rows leave with the field they write into whenever the preview is the only pane showing. The text size preference reaches the preview and deliberately not the field: it is how large the lyrics are read from across a room, and the field holds the source of the file rather than the song (scaling it would also move the columns of a tab away from the width the monospaced font keeps them at). The Chords switch does not reach the preview's lyrics: it is a way of reading a song, and a preview that hid the chords being typed opposite it would answer an edit with nothing. Nor does it or the Metronome switch reach the preview's playing line, which names the key, the capo, the tempo and the time signature being typed whatever either switch says (`SongPreview` passes `shouldShowChords = true` and no `shouldShowTempo`), since the preview shows what is being written. The two panes are `weight(1f)` and not `fillMaxSize()`: a `Column` measures a weightless child against an unbounded height, and a song longer than the screen then lays the field out past the bottom edge of it instead of scrolling inside it. `ChordProOutputTransformation` colours the text from `ChordProHighlighter`'s tokens in the viewer's own colours, so a chord looks like a chord on both sides of the divider. A directive line whose value cannot be read (`{time: 5/7}`, `{capo: second}`) is drawn whole in the error colour, since the song comes out without it. The field runs that transformation for every caret move as well as for every edit, so the tokens are kept by `ChordProTokenCache` and only worked out again once the text has changed. The screen reports the text to the view model as it is typed without saving it (`onEditorTextChanged`), which is what `hasUnsavedEditorChanges` is measured from — a field nobody has typed in follows its file when that changes, so only text of the user's own is ever unsaved: every way back out of a screen goes through `CampfireViewModel.navigateBack`, so that is where an editor with unsaved text is caught and the `UnsavedChanges` dialog (save / discard / cancel) is asked instead of popping. The draft is also what the dialog saves, which is why it lives in the view model rather than here. A draft reopened on launch is unsaved like any other, so the update gate, the way out and the web's `beforeunload` treat it the same. The dialog's Save leaves only once the write has reported success (`saveEditorChangesAndLeave`): until then the editor holds the only copy of the text, so a write that fails takes the dialog away and leaves the editor, the text and the ordinary `SaveFailed` message — and a desktop window that was being closed stays open. A first read that fails shows the details screen's failed state, with Retry and Close, instead of the loading indicator.
